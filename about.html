About PicToTexts

The story behind the free picture to text converter.

PicToTexts.com was created with one simple goal: to make converting a picture to text fast, free and private for everyone — whether you're a student digitizing lecture notes at midnight or a small-business owner processing a stack of receipts.

We noticed that most OCR tools online either hide their best features behind paywalls, force you to create an account, limit how many images you can convert per day, or quietly upload your files to their servers and keep them for an unspecified time. We believed there was a better way.

What Makes Us Different

Your images never leave your device. PicToTexts runs entirely in your browser. When you upload a picture, the OCR engine processes it locally using WebAssembly technology — your images are never sent to us or anyone else. The sensitive documents, ID cards, private notes and screenshots you convert stay completely private. We couldn't read them even if we wanted to, because they never reach us.

Genuinely free. There are no daily limits, no premium tiers, no watermarks and no sign-up requirements. Whether you convert one screenshot a month or a hundred scanned pages a day, the experience is the same. The site is supported by advertising, which keeps the tool free for everyone.

No friction. No account creation, no email confirmation, no software download, no browser extension. Open the page, drop your picture, copy your text. That's the entire product.

Our Technology

PicToTexts is powered by Tesseract, one of the world's most respected open-source OCR engines. Tesseract began at HP Labs in the 1980s, was open-sourced in 2005, and was subsequently sponsored and developed by Google for over a decade. Today it supports more than 100 languages and delivers excellent accuracy on printed text.

What makes our implementation unusual is where the engine runs. Instead of running Tesseract on a server farm and shuttling your images across the internet, we package it to run directly in your web browser via WebAssembly — a technology that lets near-native code run safely inside web pages. The first conversion loads the engine and your selected language model; after that, conversions are fast and fully local.

Who We Serve

Every day, people use PicToTexts to solve very practical problems: students digitize lecture slides and textbook passages, professionals extract text from scanned contracts and receipts, translators pull foreign text from photos to run through translation tools, researchers capture quotes from archival documents, and everyday users copy text from screenshots that can't otherwise be selected. If you have text trapped inside an image, we help you set it free.
